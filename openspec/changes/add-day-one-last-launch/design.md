# Day 1 · Last Launch from Tinseltown

A mail-order alien Queen has stolen the hall's brass rocket ornament. She is using it as a mold for eggs that will hang undetected on Christmas trees. Players see her red express parcel leave a Los Angeles movie theater, follow it to a rocket depot, then ride the last mail rocket to Luna to intercept the return shipment.

## What happened in Duke Nukem 3D

The registered 1996 PC game told its story in three episodes. Later that year, the Plutonium PAK upgrade and the standalone Atomic Edition added the fourth episode, *The Birth*. Duke went to the Moon in the second episode and came back to Los Angeles for the third. The fifth episode, *Alien World Order*, came twenty years later in *World Tour*. There really was a rocket in episode one. A secret exit in *Toxic Dump* led to *Launch Facility*. Duke could enter the capsule, but the switch in the control room blew the rocket up and the level led back to *The Abyss*. Our crew gets to take the flight. Moving the later stadium and Queen fight onto Luna lets all four episodes contribute to that one journey. [3D Realms' Launch Facility walkthrough](https://www.frag-net.com/past/www.3drealms.com/duke3d/walkthrough/e1m5.html)

The original RPG fired a heavy single rocket. The double launcher was the **Devastator**, which fired rapid miniature missiles. Pipe Bombs had a separate thumb remote. The Freezer turned enemies into statues Duke could shatter, and the Shrinker made them small enough to stomp. Atomic added the **Microwave Expander**. The same curiosity applied to places. Hollywood's projector worked, its bathroom vents led somewhere, and a cracked wall could open a shortcut. ## The playable route

The times budget a first visit from Door 1 to the ornament. Optional secrets can extend it.

| Minutes | Place | What the crew does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 | **The midnight picture show, Los Angeles** | Step onto a snowy cinema roof beneath a neon sign for the *Moon Bowl*. Drop through a vent. Switching on the projector catches a courier stealing the ornament on the loading-dock camera. Boar ushers rise from the seats. Toss a remote snow cracker onto a passing prop cart and click as it passes the stage latch. A plywood Moon drops face-first and reveals the alley behind the screen. |
| 4–9 | **Burger counter and dead-letter office** | Follow the red mail sacks through the diner. Freeze a roller-skating boar and shove the ice statue across the tiles into the brass service bell. A wind-up diner mascot can take another turn if the crew clears the boars early. The shutter opens and the till prints a red dispatch ticket. The parcel belt goes through the wall into the post office. Its ticket reader opens the freight lift the crew passed on the way in. |
| 9–14 | **The twin silos** | The freight lift rises into gantry lights. A boar foreman in a wind-up loader blocks the catwalk while jetpack couriers swoop between the silos. The hall's Snow Rocket knocks away his marked cardboard armor in one broad burst. The first rocket lifts off with the red sack. Stamp the crew's ticket **OVERNIGHT**, board the reserve rocket and pull the launch handle. |
| 14–17 | **The mail flight** | The cabin remains walkable. L.A. recedes through the portholes and Earth comes into view. There are about 30 seconds of ascent, time to open a rattling mail locker and meet the first wind-up hatchling, then a short loading-bay fight at the dock. Walk down the ramp into gray dust. Give players a few quiet seconds to see Earth over the stadium lights. |
| 17–24 | **Lunar customs and incubator** | Find the shrink ray at an oversize-parcel desk. Shrink a charging customs guard to toy size and stomp it. A fixed parcel sizer lets a volunteer ride the tabletop sorting railway between towering rubber stamps. Pulling its lever restores normal size and opens the freight door for everyone. Behind the glass the Postmistress Queen lifts the brass original from its mold. The copies hatch. Follow her delivery belt through the players' tunnel. |
| 24–30 | **The Moon Bowl** | A midfield lift brings the crew up under floodlights. The crowd is cardboard. One alien works the applause sign. The Queen skates between the uprights laying ornament eggs. Twin Tinsel Launchers alternate quick shots to clear the hatchlings. Dodge her charge so she hits a goalpost and exposes the stage latch. Send a remote snow cracker down the sideline cart and click under the latch. The plywood Moon drops like a lid on the Queen's shipping crate. The scoreboard reads **RETURN TO SENDER**. Recover the brass rocket at midfield and walk home through the tunnel. |

The cinema promises the Moon Bowl on its marquee, and the twin gantries rise above the alley. On Luna the same team logo appears on parcels all the way to midfield. Someone who misses a line of dialogue can still follow the chase.

Two optional discoveries reward friends who poke at things. Flushing a cinema toilet rings the service desk, and a furious boar posts an **OUT OF ORDER** sign on the stall. At lunar concessions a button marked **FAMILY SIZE** expands one popcorn kernel until it pushes open a hidden viewing box, an Atomic Expander joke. Through its window, Earth sits exactly between the goalposts.

## Co-op, controls and pacing

- A solo player can operate every switch. At customs, friends can stay full-size to hold the freight lane while one takes the miniature railway. Both routes meet at the same door. With a larger crew, add couriers on a second approach and split eggs between the sidelines. Keep the boss's charge-and-crash pattern readable.
- Checkpoint the gantry, lunar dock and stadium entrance. Let downed players return there. A missed flight leaves an express-dispatch hatch on Earth, and friends entering Door 1 late arrive at the crew's current checkpoint with its unlocked tools. Each participant can leave for the hall independently.
- Use the existing Fire and Use controls. A fresh Fire press throws a cracker and the next deliberate press clicks that player's remote. Refill it at both stage carts so a mistimed click is cheap and funny. For Quest and reduced-motion play, keep the cabin floor and horizon steady. Use porthole scenery for flight and let the miniature railway be walked alongside. Caption the bell, countdown and boss charge and give each a visible cue.
- The first-run target is 25–35 minutes on ordinary difficulty. Record blind solo and group runs, including an eight-player session. If the diner key loop runs long, make the freight lift visible from the till. If the finale runs long, shorten the wait for the Queen's next charge. Preserve the quiet first view of Earth.

## Integration and release

The production catalog currently has no missions. The test fixture advances a shared counter. Hall shooting currently accepts shots only in hall space, and the sample mission has small fixed movement bounds. The full level needs pack-specific movement, fire, encounter updates and a snapshot for friends arriving late. Reuse the existing run, engine networking, objective, independent return and personal-ornament paths. Put the route, timings and enemy behavior in the external Day 1 Lua pack. Use original voxel props, sprite enemies, signage, score and voices.

Keep a single authoritative record of the launch, checkpoint, stage latches, Queen phase and completed award. Friends can pop only their own crackers. A simultaneous finish advances the run once. For the later playable release, ship the pack and matching catalog revision together on the client and room service. The previous matching release remains a rollback point.
